Several Agent15-derived notes describe MAIL-06 as saying the standby suffix `5E67` “might help identify the book.” That sentence is **not present** in the supplied original `resources/evidence/mail/06.eml`.

In the local source, this is lines 19–21. MAIL-06 identifies:

MAIL-09 separately says that a map-back book in the television room belongs to David and that a tea receipt is being used as a bookmark (original `resources/evidence/mail/09.eml`, lines 19–21). The common first name/owner gives a possible same-person association. The supplied sources do not establish that David's WN-14 pocket book and the MAIL-09 map-back book are the same physical object.

The appearance of ticket ST-094 and p.94 in candidate book editions is a possible coincidence or selector hypothesis. No source-defined rule maps the standby WN-14 suffix to page 94 / word 103, and no source currently connects that suffix to the MAIL-09 book.

Accordingly:
- Do not describe MAIL-06 as explicitly linking `5E67` to the book.
- Do not use `5E67` as evidence that any particular title/edition is David's map-back book.
- Keep the page-94/word-103 construction below the direct MAIL-09 physical-book lead and the primary Emily WN-14 desk-note/envelope source.
