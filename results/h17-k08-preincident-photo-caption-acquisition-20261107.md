# K08 pre-incident shared photo-caption acquisition rule

**Agent 49 · 7 November 2026**

## Finding

MAIL-11 is dated **1 October 2026, 18:14 UTC**, two days before the 3 October incident. Emily explicitly says that desk notes and photograph captions were how she worked out the final desk phrase. This establishes that the photo-caption material she referred to was already known to her before the incident. The relevant acquisition scope must therefore include pre-incident crew/galley/noticeboard photographs and captions, not only images taken during the incident.

The surviving note extract also shows that the photo/caption material was not necessarily authored by Emily:

| N01 | Jason | Empty-bench photograph |
| N08 | Jason | Window/water photograph |
| N09 | Zoey | Galley-board first-cup caption |

MAIL-14 (2 October 2026, 14:38 UTC) independently says the photographs and galley captions were kept separately from the engineering procedure, while Emily's desk notes were under the WN-14 folder. MAIL-08 (30 September) refers to gland-residue engineering photographs; keep that distinct from the personal/crew/galley photo-caption class.

The surviving N01 transcription gives Jason's caption as “The sea looks different when you have finally finished your tea.” N10 later says Emily kept returning to “the photograph after the last cup.” The sea/tea/last-cup connection is a contextual locator only; the literal N01 caption is not the accepted K08 phrase. Prior predecessor analysis treats N01 as the N10-linked photo antecedent: Emily's N10 reference to the photograph after the last cup is contextually paired with Jason's empty-bench photograph in N01. This is a cross-author contextual link, not proof that the two notes describe the same physical image or that Emily adopted Jason's caption. Prioritize the original N01 image and caption as a testable candidate, while retaining N02/N06 as the Emily-authored exact-language controls.

## New retrieval discriminator

Request the original **shared crew / noticeboard / galley photograph and caption archive** with author and edit attribution intact, including material created or captioned before 1 October 2026. Do not restrict the search to Emily-owned images or to photographs first taken on 3 October. Preserve:

- original image files, negatives/scans where present, and all sidecars;
- any original physical prints, both front and back, including reverse-side handwriting, lab/date stamps, crop marks, adhesive/attachment traces and the print's position in its original album/board sequence;
- EXIF/IPTC/XMP metadata and original file timestamps;
- album, noticeboard or galley-board membership and folder path;
- caption text, author/editor identity, revision history and caption timestamps;
- links between a photo, its caption, the WN-14 folder and any later envelope/margin record;
- custody, export and profile-migration provenance.

The FA-18 local archival-station/profile route and the MAIL-14 separate-photo/caption locator are possible custody paths; neither proves that a particular image or caption is the one Emily used.

## Predeclared positive and negative outcomes

A useful positive is an original pre-incident caption with defensible provenance showing that Emily used or adapted it into the final incident-envelope margin. The caption's existence before MAIL-11 is not enough by itself; the source must connect it to the final margin or to an authenticated version history.

A generic late-watch, sea, window, lamp, table or first-cup caption is only an antecedent candidate. It is not K08 without that source join. A 3 October engineering photograph, including the gland-residue material in MAIL-08, does not satisfy this discriminator unless a separate record explicitly links it to Emily's final caption.

## Limits

The N-records do not carry individual dates, so their note sequence is not an authenticated photo chronology. MAIL-11 establishes that relevant captions were already known by 1 October 18:14, but it does not quote the final phrase, identify a unique photograph, or prove that a particular N-record is the caption Emily used. MAIL-20 (4 October) still establishes that the exact finally selected wording matters; no final wording has been recovered here.

This is a source-selection and acquisition rule only. **New exact K08 words: 0.** It does not authorize another phrase-generation sweep or reopen the closed 21 October custodian channel.
