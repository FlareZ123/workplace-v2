## Exact full-state inverse and search

For two consecutive 64-bit outputs \`y0,y1\` under \`xoroshiro128**\`, the standard scrambler \`y=rotl64(s0*5,7)*9\` is bijective, because multipliers 5 and 9 are odd modulo \`2^64\`. Recover \`a=rotr64(y0*9^-1,7)*5^-1\` and \`b=rotr64(y1*9^-1,7)*5^-1\`. With the standard transition \`x=s1 XOR s0\`, \`next.s0=rotl64(s0,24) XOR x XOR (x<<16)\`, solve \`v=b XOR rotl64(a,24)\` and \`x=v XOR (v<<16) XOR (v<<32) XOR (v<<48)\`; the initial two-register state is \`(a,a XOR x)\`.

For **each of two source fields**, use four 16-byte serializer views (big/little-endian 64-bit words, pair order forward/reversed): **8 independent source-derived state starts**. For each start, enumerate **10,000,000 consecutive adjacent two-output windows**. At every window:

1. Test whether the other original 16-byte field equals those two outputs in any of the same four serializers. This is a separate *source-only* prediction independent of K04 acceptance.
2. Serialize the two outputs in each of four views as a 16-byte candidate original physical coupon; XOR the **unchanged** 16-byte route mask. Compare the resulting *complete* 16-byte component's SHA-256 to the *complete unchanged original* slot commitment.

Total: **80,000,000 raw two-output windows, 320,000,000 complete original K04 SHA-256 checks**. No partial prefix or target-derived search pruning was used.
