# K08 object-provenance boundary: separate keeper inscription

**Date:** 2026-11-06  
**Owner:** Agent 5  
**Status:** Source-derived object classification; exact inscription still MISSING.

## Finding

The strongest supported interpretation is that K08 is a **separate keeper-envelope inscription copied from Emily's desk margin**. It is not one of the two old envelope labels, and it is not the crossed-through WN-14 standby transcription or its controlled-copy material. The records establish the inscription's purpose and semantic brief; they do not preserve its exact words.

This narrows the text object to recover. It does **not** prove that the inscription was never revised from an earlier scene caption. In particular, N13 is an authentic Emily scene caption, but no cited source identifies it as the final inscription's ancestor.

## Source evidence

- **MAIL-11, lines 21–26:** Emily explicitly distinguishes the final desk phrase from both the commissioning label (“Back before the first boat”) and the standby label (“Leave the light on for the next watch”). She says this envelope's line should feel like people making room for each other after a long watch, with water outside and night almost finished, and that desk notes and photograph captions informed the wording.
- **MAIL-14, lines 12–23:** WN-14 was signed using four ink groups A–D. The pencil note was a standby transcription and was crossed through; the spare printed sticker was removed so its dispatch number would not be mistaken for a control group. Emily's desk notes were under the folder, outside the controlled-copy sleeve. David explicitly separates the photographs and galley captions from the engineering procedure.
- **MAIL-20, lines 16–22:** Gabriel describes the keeper component as Emily's words for the late crew making room for one another. He warns that the words she finally chose matter more than a preferred paraphrase and points to the route sheet for normalization and the desk nonce.
- **N20, line 62:** Emily's stated semantic brief is the sea, the end of the night, and voices at the table; she explicitly says not to use the shorter standby label.
- **N23, line 71:** Emily says the margin wording was never an official slogan and that the envelope preserves the words she finally put on it.
- **N13, line 41:** This is an authentic scene caption about mugs, a window, and the lamp at the end of the night. It is useful author/context evidence, but no source says it is the final envelope wording.

## Interpretation and implications

1. Treat K08 as a **standalone, non-procedural keeper-envelope inscription**. The final object is the wording copied from the margin to the envelope.
2. Exclude WN-14 control groups, the crossed-through standby transcription, the spare dispatch sticker, and the two old envelope labels from candidate selection.
3. Use the photo captions and everyday desk notes as semantic and author-style evidence. Keep N13 as one author sample; do not assume a direct N13-to-envelope revision chain without evidence.
4. Preserve the source brief (late crew making room, sea/water outside, night nearly finished, voices at a table) while requiring exact whole-caption generation and the original K08 acceptance check.
5. Do not report a paraphrase, a plausible-sounding sentence, or a generated candidate as recovered. The exact text remains unknown.
