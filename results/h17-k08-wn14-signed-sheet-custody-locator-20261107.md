# WN-14 signed-sheet and photocopy custody locator

**Question.** Does the current K08 acquisition plan identify all physical WN-14 source objects, or does it conflate the signed register, Emily's desk notes and the folder passed to incoming watch?

## Source facts

The surviving messages describe at least three distinct physical locations or objects:

1. **Original ordinary duty sheet under a clear cover.** MAIL-03 says the WN-14 ink entries came from the ordinary duty sheet, distinct from the standby sheet with a grey diagonal line. The original was left under a clear cover rather than punched; its top label was lifting, and the scanner had clipped the right edge. The four groups follow letter order A/B/C/D even though the printer placed them in two columns (MAIL-03, lines 14–27).
2. **Two photocopies in Emily's tray.** MAIL-06 supplies the signed B and D values, says pencil alternatives were crossed out before signing, distinguishes the pocket-book standby transcription ending 5E67 from the signed group D value, and says both photocopies would be left in Emily's tray. It also says the ink sheet should be kept flat because a fold runs through the second box (MAIL-06, lines 14–24).
3. **WN-14 folder and Emily's loose desk notes.** MAIL-14 confirms the four ink groups were signed in A/B/C/D order with no replacements. It says the pencil standby note was crossed through, a spare printed sticker was removed from the cover, the folder would be handed to incoming watch, and Emily's desk notes were under that folder outside the controlled-copy sleeve. Photographs and galley captions were kept separate from the engineering procedure (MAIL-14, lines 12–23).

MAIL-03 also explicitly distinguishes the ordinary duty sheet from a separate standby sheet bearing a grey diagonal line. Do not assume that this grey-line sheet is identical to the pocket-book standby transcription ending 5E67. These statements do not establish that the original register sheet, either photocopy, the standby sheet, the WN-14 folder and Emily's desk-note stack travelled together. The original sheet's later custody is unresolved. The folder handoff is a separate event from the photocopies being left in Emily's tray.

## Gap in the current acquisition list

The K08 section of `h17-custodian-acquisition-addendum-20261107.md` requests Emily's WN-14 desk-note stack and original margin wording, plus the original envelope and photographs/captions. It does not explicitly request the original ordinary duty sheet under the clear cover or both photocopies in Emily's tray as separate objects. The Priority 3 book section requests the 5E67 standby transcription as a locator, but does not request the complete pocket book or distinguish its standby note from the signed register.

This omission matters because the signed sheet and its copies are the only source-located physical records that preserve the complete A/B/C/D WN-14 group set and its original layout. They may carry a title, legend, marginal instruction, annotation, or other provenance information relevant to interpreting the accepted K01 bytes. That possibility is unverified. None of MAIL-03/06/14 says the bytes are page/word coordinates or gives a book-cipher instruction. The signed sheet does not itself supply Emily's final K08 phrase.

## Retrieval and preservation priority

If an authorized retrieval window reopens, treat these as separate objects and preserve their as-found relationships:

- Original ordinary duty sheet carrying the WN-14 ink groups, under its clear cover in the night-folder context. Photograph the clear cover, lifting top label, right edge, both faces, two-column layout, letter labels, fold through the second box, margins, verso and any annotations before lifting or flattening it. Record the original folder, register and custodian identifiers.
- Both photocopies from Emily's tray, front and back, preserving order, paper orientation, toner/scan clipping, annotations and any differences from the original.
- The WN-14 folder handed to incoming watch, the loose desk notes beneath it, and the controlled-copy sleeve as distinct layers. Record who received the folder, when, where the loose notes were found and whether any photocopy was inside or outside it.
- The separate standby sheet with the grey diagonal line mentioned in MAIL-03, preserved in its as-found position and clearly distinguished from the ordinary duty sheet.
- David's pocket book containing the old standby transcription ending 5E67, retained only as a clearly marked non-authoritative comparison object.
- The spare printed sticker removed from the cover, the lifting top label and the loose label pile that included a label from the previous month's drill. Preserve separately as exclusion controls; MAIL-14 says the sticker's dispatch number is not one of the control groups.

Keep the signed ink sheet flat as requested in MAIL-06. Do not remove fasteners, rub pencil marks, apply chemicals, or separate layers before high-resolution photography and qualified document-examiner assessment. Preserve any folds, impressions, pencil crossings, adhesive residue and underlays. Obtain the night-folder, tray and incoming-watch custody records where they survive.

## Research boundary

The signed values and A/B/C/D order are source-supported by MAIL-03/06/14. The physical custody locations above are source-reported, while the later location of the original sheet and any relationship between the photocopies and the folder remain uncertain. The standby value 5E67 is explicitly not the signed group-D value. Do not use it as a recovery key.

This is a physical-source retrieval lead, not a K08 recovery. Do not perform page/word extraction, substitute a different book or edition, or infer a page-word interpretation from the K01 bytes until the exact David-owned book and edition, and any original selector instruction, are independently identified. The exact K08 phrase must still be attributed to Emily's final envelope wording and pass the unchanged acceptance HMAC.
