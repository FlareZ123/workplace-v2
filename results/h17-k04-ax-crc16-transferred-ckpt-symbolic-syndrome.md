## New hypothesis and recovery relevance

Earlier AX-7778 and future AX checkpoint studies interpreted each 16-bit checkpoint as a **direct upper or lower 16-bit half** of a native/tempered MT19937 word. A printer/transfer system might instead retain a **CRC16 checksum of one 32-bit output word**, encoding information from all 32 native bits. A sufficiently indexed future checkpoint burst in this different representation could constrain the 128 unknown selected native K04 bits within the conditional p951 shared-MT generator model. This test asks whether the original AX sequences support that alternate encoder before using them as donor keys.

The source stage geometry and rank are inherited from [Agent 49's exact source basis](../agents/agent49/k04_p951_symbolic_basis.py), where original spool row129 is excluded. The original printer source retains rank **15,365** and all 128 selected coupon bits remain unconstrained. The output-encoding change is distinct from [Agent17's direct halfword parity syndrome](h17-k04-ax-halfword-parity-syndrome-forward-stage.md) and [Agent7's additive-salt halfword test](h17-k04-ax-modadd-checkpoint-stage-holdout.md). Nothing in the documents authenticates historical CRC16 encoding of transfer checkpoints, so this is a finite experimental producer model.

## Consequence

The direct-halfword finding at stage−157 is retained, but **these two standard CRC16-on-one-32bit-word checkpoint encodings do not supply an authenticated future native-word donor** anywhere in the stage-wide stride-one, minimum-16-parity-information scope. This is a new serializer/observation hypothesis rejection, not proof that checkpoints cannot encode MT state through a different operation. Before scaling to variable strides, arbitrary polynomials or multiword CRC models, identify a physical/source reason for a specific encoder family and test a held-out real transfer. The true K04 raw coupon still has **0 authenticated original bits**, and K08 terminal phrase **0 independently quoted words**.
