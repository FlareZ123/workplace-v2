### K08: indented writing surviving on adjacent contemporary desk paper

Original `resources/evidence/mail/14.eml` states David placed **Emily's desk notes under the signed WN-14 folder, outside the controlled-copy sleeve** on 2 October. Original crew note **N23** says Emily's *finally chosen* keeper-envelope wording was in a **margin**. MAIL11 and MAIL20 establish that the drafts and photograph captions do not quote the final text. The missing original may therefore have been **physically handwritten** on an envelope or margin while other contemporary sheets were nearby.

**New possible surviving record class:** indented handwriting can be impressed onto **underlying sheets in a paper stack** and persist after the inscribed top page is lost. A qualified forensic document examiner could examine **already preserved, correctly dated original paper** from Emily's desk/folder/envelope stack (if any survives), for the terminal margin's pressure impression, preserving chain of custody and order. This is a different carrier from the previously proposed photo thumbnail, filename index, editorial sidecar, direct margin photograph, or phrase generator. An [established forensic description](https://www.microtrace.com/recovering-indented-writing-by-esda/) explains how ESDA reveals writing impressions from removed top sheets; [NIST](https://www.nist.gov/glossary-term/22706) defines the technique. An older [Forensic Science International study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/037907389090161Q) cautions that ESDA is not always strictly non-destructive for graphite/pencil inscriptions, so examination must be managed by the custodian's qualified forensic examiner after appropriate documentation.

**Critical missing chain:** MAIL14 proves one **2 October** paper proximity relationship, **not** that the later final keeper inscription was written on top of that folder or any preserved sheets. N23 does not specify whether the margin was penned on paper, when, or what rested below it. Without independent physical stack/custody evidence, this remains a plausible forensic collection class, not an authorized exact-caption candidate. A discovered impression would require Emily/final-envelope attribution, version/date discrimination against N02/N06 drafts and N17/N18 older labels, verbatim transcription of legible words, then the original K08 normalization/HMAC and SHA256 commitment. Unreadable impressions, earlier drafts, or plausible scene language count as **zero** attested final words.
