## Why this is a distinct route

The surviving source establishes a specific paper grouping:

- MAIL-14 (2 October 2026, 14:38 UTC) places Emily's personal desk notes physically **under the WN-14 folder**, outside its controlled-copy sleeve. It distinguishes those notes from the photographs and galley captions kept separately.
- N23 says Emily put the finally chosen wording in her personal margin, but does not quote it or identify the exact sheet.
- MAIL-20 says several drafts/captions existed and that the exact final wording matters.

A visible margin, photograph or transcription is not the only possible physical witness. If Emily wrote the final margin while another sheet, pad leaf, folder insert or backing sheet was underneath, writing pressure may have left latent indentations on that underlying material. Such an underleaf could preserve some or all of the missing characters even if the top sheet or a photograph is absent.

**This is a conditional mechanism, not a source fact.** The export does not establish that an underlay was present when the final margin was written, that the writing pressure transferred, or that any relevant paper survives.

## Forensic basis

ANSI/ASB Standard 044-19, *Standard for Examination of Documents for Indentations*, is the published 2019 first edition listed in the NIST OSAC Registry. NIST describes an electrostatic detection device (EDD) as using electrostatic charge and toner distribution to visualize paper-fiber disturbances such as indentations and erasures.

The SWGDOC *Standard for Indentation Examinations* explains that writing on an upper sheet can leave impressions on sheets beneath it, including sheets in indirect contact. It also cautions that EDD/ESDA can partially lift pencil writing or single-strike ribbon typing, so adequate documentation must precede examination. Prior handling, storage and other processing can affect results.

Preserve the original sheet order, orientation, both faces, folds, clips, sleeve/folder placement and custody history. Before movement or specialist processing, photograph each item at high resolution with scale and record its original position. Do not rub, shade, pencil over, flatten, clean, chemically process or reorder the historical papers.

A qualified forensic document examiner should assess the material under the current applicable document-indentation standard. Optical side/oblique lighting can be considered as an initial observation; the examiner should decide whether EDD/ESDA is appropriate. Because EDD may affect pencil marks, full documentation must precede it. Preserve raw photographs, examination settings, any EDD lifts, hashes and an independent verbatim transcription, including uncertain or unreadable characters. Do not treat an EDD lift as a substitute for the original paper.

An impression of a draft is evidence of that draft only. A nearby impression with no defensible source attribution is not K08.

A negative examination applies only to the specific preserved sheets examined. It does not prove that no underlay existed or that no impression survives elsewhere.

## Operational examination sequence from the published standard

The original report identified the correct examination route. The following details make the custodian request executable while preserving the paper:

1. **Intake and preservation first.** Retain the envelope, N23 page, WN-14 folder, loose notes, pad leaves, inserts, backing sheets and any shelf card in their found order and orientation. Record item IDs, both faces, folds, staples, clips, sleeves, contact surfaces and chain of custody. Do not separate stapled or attached pages, remove fasteners, flatten, rub, shade, clean or chemically process them without the responsible custodian's authorization.
2. **Qualified examiner assesses suitability.** ANSI/ASB 044-19 requires assessment of factors such as prior processing, original versus copy, printing process, writing instrument and substrate. The examiner should avoid degrading or changing impressions and obtain permission before permanent changes such as staple removal or page separation.
3. **Optical examination before EDD.** Examine both sides under lighting from multiple angles and directions, with magnification as needed. Record visible impressions and fiber disturbances using high-resolution still images or video with a moving light source. If the substrate is unsuitable for EDD, rely on optical methods and report that limitation.
4. **Pencil and other examinations are a sequencing issue.** A 2025 Ohio BCI laboratory manual warns that ESDA static charge can lift pencil particles and lower contrast; its procedure photographs/copies pencil-written documents before processing. It also notes that some fingerprint/DNA solvents can prevent ESDA images. These are laboratory-specific instructions, so the receiving examiner must coordinate the sequence with any latent-print, DNA, ink or other examiner and follow the lab's current validated procedure.
5. **EDD/ESDA only if the examiner approves.** Follow the laboratory's humidification and equipment protocol, develop and document a control indentation, process both sides where appropriate, and preserve the lift and high-resolution images. Record settings, controls, limitations and every sheet processed.
6. **Report a bounded negative.** A negative result should say that no decipherable impressions were detected on the named sheets using the specified methods. It must not be reported as proof that no underlay or contact ever existed.

This sequence is grounded in ANSI/ASB Standard 044, First Edition (2019), especially §§5.3–5.7; the Ohio BCI Questioned Documents Methods Manual, Revision 15 (effective 26 June 2025), §§4.4–4.7; and the Centre for Forensic Sciences indentation factsheet. The BCI manual is an example of one laboratory's procedure, not a universal SOP. The CFS factsheet notes that paper thickness, storage, pressure and the number/thickness of intervening layers affect recovery, and that absence of decipherable impressions does not prove that documents were never in contact.
