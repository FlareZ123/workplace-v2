# K08 physical-book locator: *The Way That I Went* candidate

**Status:** strong source-location hypothesis; not a K08 phrase recovery.

## Question

Can the unnamed map-bearing book mentioned in MAIL-09 be identified, and do the L-29 ticket numbers point into it?

## Source evidence

MAIL-09 says the television-room book with a map in the back belongs to David rather than the communal shelf. It also mentions a tea receipt being used as a bookmark and deliberately left in place. MAIL-09 does not give the title, author, edition, or bookmark page.

A strong bibliographic match is Robert Lloyd Praeger's *The Way That I Went: An Irishman in Ireland*:

- The National Library of Ireland catalogue records the 1937 first edition, 394 pages, with plates and maps: https://catalogue.nli.ie/Record/vtls000044069
- A rare-book catalogue describes the 1937 first edition as including a large folding map at the rear (Catalogue 144, item 243): https://www.deburcararebooks.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/Catalogue-144.pdf
- A separate bookseller record describes both the 1937 first edition and 1939 second edition as having a folding map at the rear: https://www.abebooks.it/ricerca-libro/titolo/way-irishman-ireland/autore/praeger/
- Google Books exposes edition-specific contents. The 1947 third edition starts **The Volcanic North** at p.62 and **The Silurian Region** at p.118: https://books.google.com/books?id=uT5RAQAAIAAJ
- The 1997 reprint starts those sections at p.59 and p.111 respectively: https://books.google.com/books?id=Fo0iAQAAIAAJ

The title, Irish geographic organization, and rear folding map make this a much closer physical match to MAIL-09 than the thematic maritime-fiction candidates.

## Ticket-page alignment

The ten supplier-return ticket IDs, read as page numbers, are:

| Printer set | Ticket pages | 1947 third-edition sections |
|---|---|---|
| N | 81, 94, 99, 106, 112 | All five fall in *The Volcanic North* (p.62–117) |
| S | 121, 130, 135, 142, 151 | All five fall in *The Silurian Region* or later sections (p.118 onward) |

That is an exact N/S regional split in the 1947 pagination.

The 1997 reprint has different section starts. Four N pages (81, 94, 99, 106) fall in *The Volcanic North* (p.59–110); ST-112 is just inside *The Silurian Region* (from p.111). All five S pages remain in the Silurian Region or later southern sections. The 1937 first edition is 394 pages, like the 1997 reprint, but its exact section-start pagination has not been established from a first-edition contents page. Do not silently apply the 1947 page boundaries to a 1937 copy.

The page-number alignment is therefore **edition-sensitive** and is evidence for checking this book, not proof that the tickets are book-cipher coordinates. The N/S split remains strong in either known contents layout; the 1997-style layout puts the final N ticket, ST-112 (part `catch`), just across the regional boundary.

## Tests and limitations

The local export contains the MAIL-09 reference but no book scan, book catalogue entry, receipt image, or margin photograph. The 1937/1939 book's full text and page images were not available through the public catalogue sources consulted. No printed-text page/word extraction or K08 HMAC candidate has been established.

A separate trial of Bram Stoker's *The Mystery of the Sea* was downgraded: its chapter titles gave thematic overlap, but simple chapter/page word and sentence selectors did not produce a K08 commitment hit, and the available digital image inventory did not support the map-at-rear clue. Do not continue that branch on motif similarity alone.

## Highest-value next evidence

If the physical source becomes available through authorized custody, establish the exact title and edition first. Then photograph:
1. the cover, title page, edition/imprint page, and rear map;
2. the tea receipt bookmark in place, including its printed date/merchant/amount and the page it marks;
3. the full marked page and adjacent pages, with both margins visible;
4. any handwritten notes or underlining on ticket pages 81, 94, 99, 106, 112, 121, 130, 135, 142 and 151.

The key question is whether the receipt or marginalia connects the book to Emily's final margin-to-envelope wording in N23. The book's printed text may be incidental. No K08 phrase, component bytes, or acceptance result is claimed here.
