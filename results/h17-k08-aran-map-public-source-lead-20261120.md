# K08 exploratory lead: Aran Islands map in Baddeley's 1911 guide

**Status: unverified source lead; not a recovered K08 phrase or authenticated carrier.**

## Public-source observations

1. A 2015 article on the Aran Islands reproduces/describes a colored map titled **“Aran Islands or Ara na Naive (The Arans of the Seas)”**, attributed to *Baddeley's Thorough Guide Ireland, Part II* (1911). The article describes the sea as pale green and the islands in yellow/brown/green. It also reproduces a separate black-and-white map from the end of Robert Lloyd Praeger's *The Way That I Went* (1937). Source: https://revistas.usp.br/abei/article/view/182368
2. A public Internet Archive OCR scan exists for C. S. Ward, *Ireland (Part II): East, West, and South including Dublin and Howth*, fifth edition (1906). Its title page identifies 26 maps and plans by Bartholomew; its Aran Islands section begins at printed page 178. Source: https://archive.org/stream/irelandpartiieas00ward/irelandpartiieas00ward_djvu.txt
3. Public bookseller/catalogue records identify a 1911 sixth-edition Part II by Baddeley/Ward with numerous maps and folding plans. The exact 1911 scan and the specific map image at original resolution have not yet been obtained.

## Why this is worth a bounded check

The K08 evidence contains a source-local “A TEAL BIT” / “LOW BIT” chain, a map-back-book reference (MAIL-09), and a note describing a line that should hold the sea, the end of the night, and voices at a table. A colored Aran map with a pale-green sea is a plausible image-carrier candidate. The map also connects naturally to the Aran literary references already present in the public-source trail.

This is **not proof** that David's book was the 1911 guide, that the map is the intended carrier, or that the map contains steganographic data. The black-and-white Praeger map is a distinct competing candidate. The 1906 Ward OCR is not a substitute for the 1911 map image.
