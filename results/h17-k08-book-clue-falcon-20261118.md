# K08 book-clue check: *The Falcon on the Baltic* (2026-11-18)

## Question

Whether the surviving desk-book clue can identify the source intended by the K08 page/word hypothesis, and whether a direct WN-14 page/word extraction from a public scan produces the committed phrase.

## Evidence

The local export's MAIL-09 says the library book "with the map in the back" belongs to David. A public Internet Archive record for E. F. Knight's *The Falcon on the Baltic* identifies a 1896 Longmans edition with 308 printed pages and one foldout; the same record exposes OCR/full-text and page-number assets. A separate public transcription preserves the book text.

The accepted K01 value is documented elsewhere in the repository as WN-14. A prior hypothesis maps its eight 16-bit pairs to page/word coordinates:

- 4 / 154
- 171 / 46
- 28 / 222
- 122 / 130
- 205 / 124
- 96 / 35
- 67 / 3
- 201 / 26

I checked the public 1896 scan directly where its OCR exposes the relevant printed pages. In page-sorted order, the recoverable words are:

- p.4, w.154 -> **all**
- p.28, w.222 -> **perhaps**
- p.67, w.3 -> **at**
- p.96, w.35 -> **A**
- p.122, w.130 -> **quay**
- p.171, w.46 -> **for**

The resulting partial sequence is all perhaps at A quay for ..., which is not a coherent phrase and does not justify using this edition as the K08 source under the naive page/word interpretation.

## Conclusion

*The Falcon on the Baltic* is a substantially stronger book candidate than an arbitrary public-domain title because the export explicitly singles out a book with a rear map and the public edition has a foldout. However, the direct WN-14 page/word interpretation does **not** yield the K08 phrase on the 1896 scan.

Treat the book identity as a lead, not as a recovered K08. The next useful step is edition-faithful extraction from the actual 1889/1890s printing (or the exact David copy), not another round of absolute-word-offset guesses against generic transcriptions.
