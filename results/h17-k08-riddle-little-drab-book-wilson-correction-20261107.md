# K08 book-identity correction: the Riddle's Little Drab Book is separate from Cowper

**Date:** 2026-11-07  
**Author:** Agent 7  
**Status:** evidence-backed correction to the book-candidate ranking; exact physical copy still unverified

## Question

Does the "map-back book" clue in the surviving H-17 notes identify Cowper's *Sailing Tours*, or does the literary clue point to a different yachtsman's guide?

## Method

Re-read the relevant "Little Drab Book" passage in Erskine Childers's *The Riddle of the Sands* and compared the narrative sequence with the book candidates already present in the K08 research. Checked public bibliographic descriptions for the real-world Wilson guide.

## Evidence

1. In the novel, Cowper's *Sailing Tours* is listed among several books in Davies's library. The narrative then introduces a separate "particular volume" and describes it as an old, cheap-cloth guide for yachtsmen to a British estuary. Its title page is attributed to "Lieut. X—, R.N."; Dollmann's name is said to be on its back; and it has a photographic frontispiece showing a yacht and its owner. The sequence makes the guidebook a different object from Cowper's *Sailing Tours*.
   - Full-text source: https://www.ajhw.co.uk/books/book160/book160.html
   - PDF text source: https://www.metrici.com/ebta/titles/the_riddle_of_the_sands/the_riddle_of_the_sands_pdf.pdf

2. A real-world book with a particularly close subject/title match is W. Eric Wilson's *The Pilot's Guide to the Thames Estuary and the Norfolk Broads for Yachtsmen*, first published in 1934. A first-edition bookseller description reports 411 pages, 18 folding charts, 20 photographic illustrations, and brown cloth binding:
   - Bibliographic/seller record: https://www.abebooks.co.uk/first-edition/Pilots-Guide-Thames-Estuary-Norfolk-Broads/84799720/bd
   - A separate 1938 second-edition listing describes the guide's Thames Estuary and Norfolk Broads coverage: https://www.abebooks.co.uk/Pilots-Guide-Thames-Estuary-Norfolk-Broads/12309174396/bd

3. The Wilson guide is a plausible real-world analogue for the H-17 map-back yachtsman's guide clue. It is not proven to be the exact book from David's desk. It postdates the 1903 novel, and the novel's fictional "Lieut. X—, R.N." attribution does not identify W. Eric Wilson.

4. The earlier Cowper investigation remains reproducible as a separate experiment. The Part II word-103 extraction in binder-tab order is solent yards sailing the even. This fragment does not establish a K08 phrase, has not passed the K08 HMAC, and should not be treated as the primary map-back-book route. The Part I HathiTrust item is full view but automated page retrieval was blocked; no more automated HathiTrust/Google Books probes are warranted under the existing research direction.

5. The 5E67 → page 94 / word 103 split remains a low-confidence hypothesis. The ticket page numbers all fit within the 411-page Wilson first edition, but that compatibility alone does not establish a source-defined book cipher. No Wilson p.94 scan or exact David-owned copy has been obtained.

## Conclusion

The K08 book-candidate ranking should change:

- **Primary physical-profile candidate:** David's exact map-back yachtsman's guide, with Wilson's 1934 *Pilot's Guide* as the strongest currently identified real-world analogue.
- **Secondary experiment:** Cowper's *Sailing Tours* Part II page-word fragment. Preserve the images, OCR and extraction, but do not promote it as the Little Drab Book.
- **Still unresolved:** the exact edition and physical copy, any map-verso inscription, the tea-receipt bookmark, Emily's final envelope wording, and the K08 HMAC.

This correction changes the source-identification hypothesis. It does not recover K08.
