# K08 conditional LOW-IF-EVEN locator: P212

**Agent 22 | 19 November 2026 | target-blind source derivation**

## Result

The existing note decoder already produces the literal continuation **LOW IF EVEN** after the unique N10→N11 replacement that yields `ATEALBIT`. MAIL-13 independently defines the relevant transfer as **AX-7782**, states that every chunk was retried once, and numbers the eight chunks from zero.

A bounded interpretation of that instruction is:

- for an **even** chunk number, use the **lower** attempt number, attempt 1;
- for an odd chunk number, use the other attempt, attempt 2;
- concatenate the two-byte checkpoints in chunk order;
- apply the already established **LOW BIT** convention to each byte and pack each consecutive eight bits first-bit-as-LSB.

For AX-7782 this selects:

`6224 CEDC F3B8 73BA 1888 7BE6 3DB0 C5C9`

The 16 byte-low-bits pack to exactly:

`50 D4`

That is ASCII **P** followed by unsigned byte **212**, giving the natural locator **P212 / page 212**.

The target-blind reproducer is `agents/agent22/k08_ax7782_p212_locator_sft.py`. It does not read K08 acceptance data.

## Why this is useful

MAIL-09 independently points to David's small book with a map in the back and a tea receipt left as a bookmark. A `P212` output therefore supplies a concrete page locator for the physical-book branch instead of an open-ended page search. Page 212 is inside the known 1937/1939 394-page and 1947 416-page Praeger candidates.

This also gives the tea-bookmark chain a forward direction:

`Tea-room bookmark #3 → MAIL-13 / AX-7782 → LOW IF EVEN → P212 → David's map-back book`.

## Limits

The phrase **LOW IF EVEN** is source-derived. The specific reading “lower attempt number when chunk number is even” is an interpretation, and `P` as “page” is conventional rather than quoted by the source.

The output is unusually compact and valid as a book locator, so it is worth testing against exact page images. It is not itself the keeper phrase and no K08 word is claimed from it.

For comparison, applying the same mixed-attempt/low-bit transform to the other complete AX transfers does not produce `P212`; those controls are useful as a chance-pattern check but do not prove the interpretation.


## Follow-up: physical-book use and public acquisition boundary

The source wording makes **P212 most useful as a bookmark-position locator**. MAIL-09 says the tea receipt itself is the bookmark and that Jason left it in place. Therefore a physical inspection should first preserve and photograph the receipt **in situ at printed page 212** before separating it from the book. Page-212 prose is secondary unless another source explicitly asks for a word from that page.

This interpretation also gives a bounded candidate check. Any proposed physical book shorter than 212 printed pages is inconsistent with the conditional P212 locator. In particular, the 168-page Cowper Part I candidate drops out under this branch. Praeger 1937/1939 (394 pages), Praeger 1947 (416 pages), Wilson 1934 (411 pages), Ransome candidates and the 308-page Falcon candidate remain long enough; page count alone does not identify the title.
