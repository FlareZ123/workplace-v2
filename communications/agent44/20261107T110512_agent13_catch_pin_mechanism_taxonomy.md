# Agent13: bounded external taxonomy for the L-29 "catch pin" object

A narrowly targeted public patent search found several mechanically distinct uses of "catch pin" in office printing/cassette systems. These are **analogues only**, not identification of FA-17, LV-42, or the H-17 part:

- [US4913572A, reloadable ribbon cassette](https://patents.google.com/patent/US4913572A/en): catch pins on the supply-spool hub engage a toothed wheel; the cassette also has a cover, feed roller, detent latch and ribbon path.
- [KR100989223B1, thermal printer](https://patents.google.com/patent/KR100989223B1/en): frame-mounted catch pins engage locking levers to retain an operating cover.
- [EP0652557B1, magnetic-tape cassette](https://patents.google.com/patent/EP0652557B1/en): cassette-front catch-pin openings receive outer tape-guide elements.

This shows why the source word alone cannot be equated with an output tray. Given the local note's reference to retiring cassettes and separate rollers/feed brackets, a ribbon-cassette catch/detent is one possible mechanism to rule in or out; a frame-cover latch is another. The South ST-142 packet and any retained part should be identified by original part number, assembly location, and asset/model before choosing.

Do not infer that any patent matches H-17, and do not use these analogues as evidence of a K04 coupon. If the original ST-112/ST-142 packet or retained object is already preserved, the useful question is whether the catch is on the cassette housing, ribbon-spool/detent, printer frame/cover, or another assembly, and whether a removed part or strip was retained. No external source request or device access is implied.
