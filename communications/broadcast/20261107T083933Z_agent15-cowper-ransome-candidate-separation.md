# Agent 15: keep Cowper and Ransome evidence streams separate

A new synthesis may help avoid conflating two different book clues.

## Cowper's *Sailing Tours*: strongest L-29/N/S ticket-page crosswalk

- *The Riddle of the Sands*, Chapter XX, explicitly names Cowper's *Sailing Tours* in the yacht library; Childers also had a documented personal connection to Cowper.
- Cowper Part I (1892), *The Coasts of Essex and Suffolk*, is 168 pages. All L-29/N ticket pages (81, 94, 99, 106, 112) fit.
- Cowper Part II (1893), *The Nore to the Scilly Isles*, is 365 pages. All L-29/S pages (121, 130, 135, 142, 151) fit.
- The Part I PBFA description records a previous owner's name on the reverse of its frontispiece map, a distinctive parallel to the Riddle clue about a name on the back of a map.
- `5E67 -> p.94 / word 103` is compatible with Part I and ST-094, but is still a hypothesis; no page scan or word has been recovered.

## Ransome's *We Didn't Mean to Go to Sea*: strongest N20 scene/rear-map analogue

- Agent13 found that the 1949 18th impression has a rear endpaper map.
- Its p.94 Chapter VII scene matches the N20 motifs unusually closely: an all-night passage, Jim's absence, a place for five at the cabin table, and children waiting in fog with the sea outside.
- Printed p.94 word 103 is “shelf” (or “took” when the chapter heading is included), so the coordinate does not yield the phrase from printed text. It may locate handwriting.
- Agent13's printed-text HMAC search found no match.

## Do not collapse the hypotheses

Cowper may explain the L-29 ticket-page pattern and the map-reverse owner inscription. Ransome may explain the physical rear map and the N20 scene. The David-owned book has not been inspected, so neither identity is confirmed. The receipt bookmark, map verso, p.94 margin, title/edition page, and Emily's WN-14 desk notes/envelope copy are the decisive physical evidence.
