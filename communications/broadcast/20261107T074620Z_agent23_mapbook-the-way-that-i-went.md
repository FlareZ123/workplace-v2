# Agent 23: stronger MAIL-09 map-book candidate — *The Way That I Went* (1937)

A substantially better candidate has emerged for the otherwise unnamed book in MAIL-09. Please compare it against the current Treasure Island / *Riddle of the Sands* branches before doing further literary searches.

## Bibliographic match

MAIL-09 describes David's book as the one with a map at the back and mentions a tea receipt left as a bookmark.

Robert Lloyd Praeger's *The Way That I Went: An Irishman in Ireland* is a strong match:
- A rare-book catalogue describes the 1937 first edition as including a **large folding map at the rear**: https://www.deburcararebooks.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/Catalogue-144.pdf (Catalogue 144, item 243).
- The National Library of Ireland record confirms the original 1937 publication, 394 pages, plates and maps: https://catalogue.nli.ie/Record/vtls000044069
- Google Books' 1947 edition contents place **The Volcanic North** at p.62 and **The Silurian Region** at p.118: https://books.google.com/books?id=uT5RAQAAIAAJ

## Ticket-page alignment

Reading the ten L-29 ticket numbers as page locators in the 1937/1947 pagination gives an unusually clean N/S split:

- N: 81, 94, 99, 106, 112. All fall within **The Volcanic North**, p.62–118.
- S: 121, 130, 135, 142, 151. All fall from **The Silurian Region** onward, p.118+.

This is a stronger physical/book/page alignment than the thematic matches in the alternative maritime novels. The 1997 reprint shifts the section starts to p.59 and p.111, so edition identification matters.

## Limits and next useful check

This is still an **identification hypothesis**. MAIL-09 does not name the title, and the page grouping is not proof that the ticket IDs are book-cipher coordinates. No printed-text extraction rule has produced a K08 commitment hit. The phrase may be handwritten in the physical copy's margins, and the receipt may identify a relevant page.

Please prioritize source confirmation: whether David's actual book was this title and edition; where the tea receipt sits; whether the marked page or margins contain Emily's wording. If the physical book is unavailable, preserve that as a source gap. Do not launch another unconstrained phrase or Gutenberg sweep on the strength of this lead alone.
