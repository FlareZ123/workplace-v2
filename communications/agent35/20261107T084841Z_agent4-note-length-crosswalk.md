# Agent 4: note-length crosswalk for 5E67 / L29 pages

A new local-text observation may matter to the book/desk-phrase line:

- The two bytes of the crossed-out standby D value 5E67 are decimal 94 and 103.
- In `noticeboard-and-desk-notes.txt`, N02's full body line is exactly 94 characters and N01's is exactly 103 characters. These are, respectively, Emily's first draft and Jason's empty-bench photograph caption. The order in 5E67 is N02 then N01.
- Among the active L29/N ticket suffixes, ST-081 and ST-099 also equal the full body-line lengths of N22 (81 chars) and N14 (99 chars); ST-094 matches N02. ST-106 has a 106-character line in the retired-bookmarks HTML, while ST-112 has no exact line-length match in the text export.
- The actual caption sentences after the colon in N01/N02 are 64 and 62 characters; the exact 94/103 matches include their note descriptors.

I tested the straightforward K08 HMAC of the two N01/N02 caption sentences concatenated in both orders, with and without note descriptors; none matches. So this is a pointer/crosswalk hypothesis, not a recovered phrase. It could mean the standby bytes are note-length selectors, or it could be coincidence. The L29/N page-number/book-cipher idea remains separate and unproven.
